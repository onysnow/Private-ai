### Whole areas not yet covered (10)

- **NEW: Violent extremism and hate-motivated attacks (behavioural mobilisation)**: Non-financial behavioural indicators that a person is moving toward ideologically motivated violence: 42 indicators in three groups (Mobilization: travel to carry out violent extremist activity; Preparation: surveilling potential attack targets; Motivation: expressing willingness to die for a violent extremist ideology), each labelled by type such as Intent, Tactics or Travel. Published as 'US Violent Extremist Mobilization Indicators' (2021 edition) by NCTC, FBI and DHS. *Why:* SF covers only terrorist and extremist financing (payments, crowdfunding, NPO flows). The catalogue has nothing on the observable conduct of a lone actor or small cell before a hate-motivated or extremist attack, and that is a common investigative subject. The dni.gov link redirects to archive.dni.gov, where the PDF was read. <https://archive.dni.gov/files/NCTC/documents/news_documents/Mobilization_Indicators_Booklet_2021.pdf>
- **NEW: Espionage and insider threat (national security)**: CDSE Insider Threat Potential Risk Indicators in 9 categories: Access Attributes; Professional Lifecycle and Performance; Foreign Considerations (foreign passport, frequent unofficial foreign travel); Security and Compliance Incidents; Technical Activity (unapproved USB device, transferring data to a personal or suspicious account); Criminal, Violent or Abusive Conduct; Financial Considerations (delinquent debt, unexplained affluence); Substance Abuse and Addictive Behaviours; Judgement, Character and Psychological Conditions. *Why:* No area covers spying, leaking classified material or insider theft for a foreign state. SF covers sanctions and export-control diversion, not the person on the inside. <https://www.information.marines.mil/Portals/224/Docs/BTAC/CDSE_insider_threat_indicators_job_aid.pdf>
- **NEW: Elder financial exploitation**: FinCEN Advisory FIN-2022-A002 (15 June 2022). Behavioural red flags: caregiver shows excessive interest in the person's finances, speaks for them or controls the conversation; new caretakers, relatives or friends carry out transactions without proper documentation; sudden changes to power of attorney, trusts or estate plans under apparent coercion or when the person lacks capacity; customer seems distressed, fearful or submissive; customer cannot be reached. Financial red flags: a dormant account with a large balance suddenly sees constant withdrawals; closing CDs or accounts regardless of penalties; repeated daily-maximum ATM withdrawals; uncharacteristic nonpayment for services; sudden non-sufficient-funds activity. *Why:* CY covers some scam-side flags (romance or government impersonation, draining retirement savings). It does not cover abuse by caregivers, family or people holding power of attorney. That is a distinct crime: often committed by people close to the victim, it shows up in guardianship and probate records, and it has its own official red-flag list. <https://fincen.gov/sites/default/files/advisory/2022-06-15/FinCEN%20Advisory%20Elder%20Financial%20Exploitation%20FINAL%20508.pdf>
- **NEW: Securities market abuse (manipulation and insider trading)**: FINRA Regulatory Notice 21-03 (10 Feb 2021). Manipulation: reverse or forward splits that concentrate shares among a few holders; marking the close (buying just before market close); small buys by a customer with a very large position, apparently to move the price; customers suddenly trading a thinly traded stock on both sides of the market; sell-only routing through omnibus or DVP/RVP accounts. Insider-related: officers, former officers, significant shareholders or their families trading before an announcement or a promotion campaign. Issuer and customer flags: abrupt changes of name, ticker or business model; current or past shell status; unsupported revenue or partnership claims; large deposits of thinly traded shares; several new overseas accounts depositing the same issuer's shares. *Why:* FR has only 'Hyped small-company stock with inside tips', the investor-facing side of pump-and-dump. The trading patterns that show manipulation and insider dealing are missing. <https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/21-03>
- **NEW: Corporate accounting and financial-statement fraud**: Fraud risk factors from the auditing standard (ISA/ASA 240, 'Examples of Fraud Risk Factors'), grouped as incentives or pressures, opportunities, and attitudes or rationalisations. Fraudulent financial reporting: heavy pressure on management to meet investor or analyst profit expectations; significant related-party transactions outside the normal course of business; management with an unusually high tolerance for risk. Misappropriation of assets: employees with access to valuable assets who face personal financial obligations; weak segregation of duties or independent checks; tolerance of petty theft. A US equivalent is PCAOB AU 316.85. *Why:* FR covers tax evasion, investment scams, insurance, healthcare billing and bankruptcy fraud, but not cooked books or earnings manipulation at operating companies, which are central to corporate investigations. <https://standards.auasb.gov.au/node/2056>
- **NEW: Prescription drug diversion and pill mills (pharmaceutical crime)**: DEA red flags for prescribers: cursory or no medical exams; brief visits; multiple drugs in the same class or excessive quantities; starting patients on high-dose narcotics; ignoring signs of abuse or toxicology results; coaching patients on what to say; non-medical staff choosing the drug; patient overdose deaths. Pharmacy red flags from DEA's Hills Pharmacy decision (81 FR 49816, 2016): many people bringing the same prescriptions in the same quantities from the same doctor; high cash prices for controlled substances; patients living far from the pharmacy; 'trinity' combinations (an opioid, a benzodiazepine and a muscle relaxer); doses above recognised thresholds; pattern prescribing; several household members on the same drugs. *Why:* FR covers health-insurance billing fraud and OC covers counterfeit medicines and illicit fentanyl. Nothing covers licensed doctors, pharmacies or distributors diverting legitimate controlled drugs, the core of opioid-crisis investigations. <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2016-07-28/html/2016-17721.htm>
- **NEW: Labour exploitation short of forced labour (wage theft, underpayment)**: Red flags from 'Off the Books: Practical Guidance for Business' (Migrant Justice Institute with Walk Free and the Australian Anti-Slavery Commissioner, August 2026). Misclassification: workers required to get an ABN to get or keep a job; contractors supervised like employees. Sham casual work: casual hours match permanent staff, no casual loading. Missing penalty rates, or flat rates that ignore loadings. Supplier refuses to provide payslips, timesheets or proof of payment. Wages partly paid in cash. Many workers' payslips show identical hours. Delayed pay or unexplained deductions. Shifts cut after a worker raises concerns. *Why:* HT covers ILO forced-labour indicators (debt bondage, document retention, withholding of wages). The large body of illegal but non-forced exploitation, such as misclassification and underpayment, has no indicators of its own. <https://cdn.walkfree.org/content/uploads/2026/08/06115628/GD551-off-the-books-guidance_DIGITAL_spreads.pdf>
- **NEW: Police misconduct and state violence (officer-level)**: Early intervention system indicators used to flag officers at risk of misconduct or excessive force: use-of-force incidents; officer-involved shootings; community complaints; internal disciplinary actions; reassignment history; civil suits or administrative claims against the officer; suspected or reported misconduct; vehicle pursuits and crashes. *Why:* SA covers state threats against investigators, and HR covers mass atrocities. No area holds indicators an investigator can apply to police use-of-force records, complaints and settlements, a common accountability-journalism beat. This is a summary list from the Council on Criminal Justice policing assessment, not a statutory list. <https://policing.counciloncj.org/assessing-the-evidence/xv-early-intervention-systems-eis>
- **NEW: Child sexual abuse in institutions (churches, schools, sport, care)**: Risk framework from the Australian Royal Commission into Institutional Responses to Child Sexual Abuse, 'Risk profiles for institutional child sexual abuse' (2017), in four dimensions. Situational: chances for adults to be alone with children or form close relationships with them. Vulnerability: the child's age, disability, earlier maltreatment, or a reason to stay silent. Propensity: a concentration of adults inclined to abuse, or of children with harmful sexual behaviours. Institutional: putting the institution's reputation ahead of children's protection, and a culture of not listening to children. *Why:* HT covers trafficking and commercial sexual exploitation of children, but nothing covers abuse inside institutions and their cover-ups, a major investigative genre (clergy abuse, residential schools, sports coaches). Only the media release summary was read. The full report is at https://www.childabuseroyalcommission.gov.au/getattachment/399a6b99-aa14-449e-bf6d-2d5d5beb773f/Risk-profiles-for-institutional-child-sexual-abuse and was not opened. <https://www.childabuseroyalcommission.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/2017-06/research-identifies-four-dimensions-of-risk-of-chi>
- **HR**: Early-warning risk factors for atrocity crimes: the UN Framework of Analysis for Atrocity Crimes (2014, reprinted 2018) has 14 risk factors and 143 indicators. Common factors: armed conflict or instability; record of serious violations; weakness of state structures; motives or incentives; capacity to commit atrocities; absence of mitigating factors; enabling circumstances or preparatory action; triggering factors. Specific factors: intergroup tensions and discrimination; signs of intent to destroy a protected group; signs of a widespread or systematic attack, or of a plan or policy to attack civilians; serious threats to people protected by IHL; threats to humanitarian or peacekeeping operations. *Why:* HR lists atrocity outcomes (forced displacement, attacks on civilians, starvation) and conflict-mineral flags. ## Coverage round 3 (10 October 2026)

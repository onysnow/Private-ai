## Gaps found by the coverage critics

Three agents each looked for what the catalogue misses, in a different way. Each item has a source. None of these are in the catalogue yet; adding them is the next research round.

### Whole areas not yet covered (10)

- **NEW: Violent extremism and hate-motivated attacks (behavioural mobilisation)**: Non-financial behavioural indicators that a person is moving toward ideologically motivated violence: 42 indicators in three groups (Mobilization: travel to carry out violent extremist activity; Preparation: surveilling potential attack targets; Motivation: expressing willingness to die for a violent extremist ideology), each labelled by type such as Intent, Tactics or Travel. Published as 'US Violent Extremist Mobilization Indicators' (2021 edition) by NCTC, FBI and DHS. *Why:* SF covers only terrorist and extremist financing (payments, crowdfunding, NPO flows). The catalogue has nothing on the observable conduct of a lone actor or small cell before a hate-motivated or extremist attack, and that is a common investigative subject. The dni.gov link redirects to archive.dni.gov, where the PDF was read. <https://archive.dni.gov/files/NCTC/documents/news_documents/Mobilization_Indicators_Booklet_2021.pdf>
- **NEW: Espionage and insider threat (national security)**: CDSE Insider Threat Potential Risk Indicators in 9 categories: Access Attributes; Professional Lifecycle and Performance; Foreign Considerations (foreign passport, frequent unofficial foreign travel); Security and Compliance Incidents; Technical Activity (unapproved USB device, transferring data to a personal or suspicious account); Criminal, Violent or Abusive Conduct; Financial Considerations (delinquent debt, unexplained affluence); Substance Abuse and Addictive Behaviours; Judgement, Character and Psychological Conditions. *Why:* No area covers spying, leaking classified material or insider theft for a foreign state. SF covers sanctions and export-control diversion, not the person on the inside. <https://www.information.marines.mil/Portals/224/Docs/BTAC/CDSE_insider_threat_indicators_job_aid.pdf>
- **NEW: Elder financial exploitation**: FinCEN Advisory FIN-2022-A002 (15 June 2022). Behavioural red flags: caregiver shows excessive interest in the person's finances, speaks for them or controls the conversation; new caretakers, relatives or friends carry out transactions without proper documentation; sudden changes to power of attorney, trusts or estate plans under apparent coercion or when the person lacks capacity; customer seems distressed, fearful or submissive; customer cannot be reached. Financial red flags: a dormant account with a large balance suddenly sees constant withdrawals; closing CDs or accounts regardless of penalties; repeated daily-maximum ATM withdrawals; uncharacteristic nonpayment for services; sudden non-sufficient-funds activity. *Why:* CY covers some scam-side flags (romance or government impersonation, draining retirement savings). It does not cover abuse by caregivers, family or people holding power of attorney. That is a distinct crime: often committed by people close to the victim, it shows up in guardianship and probate records, and it has its own official red-flag list. <https://fincen.gov/sites/default/files/advisory/2022-06-15/FinCEN%20Advisory%20Elder%20Financial%20Exploitation%20FINAL%20508.pdf>
- **NEW: Securities market abuse (manipulation and insider trading)**: FINRA Regulatory Notice 21-03 (10 Feb 2021). Manipulation: reverse or forward splits that concentrate shares among a few holders; marking the close (buying just before market close); small buys by a customer with a very large position, apparently to move the price; customers suddenly trading a thinly traded stock on both sides of the market; sell-only routing through omnibus or DVP/RVP accounts. The catalogue does not include it. <https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/923126/download?inline>
- **CY**: SIM-swap account takeover: sudden loss of mobile or SMS service, followed by password resets and fraudulent withdrawals *Why:* The FBI reported $68m lost in 2021. The catalogue covers unusual devices and IPs but not SIM swap. <https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2022/PSA220208>
- **IN**: Plagiarism: using another person's ideas, methods, results or words without credit *Why:* One of the three types of research misconduct in the federal definition (fabrication, falsification, plagiarism). The catalogue has image duplication and paper mills but no plagiarism indicator. <https://ori.hhs.gov/definition-misconduct>
- **IN**: Reported means that are impossible given the sample size and item granularity (GRIM/SPRITE inconsistencies) *Why:* A well-known statistical check for fabricated or misreported data. IN has only image- and text-based research-integrity flags. <https://peerj.com/preprints/2064.pdf>
- **IN**: Account that mostly amplifies (retweets or quotes) with few or no original posts, or with low posts but very high reach *Why:* Named in DFRLab's #BotSpot list ('Amplification', 'Low posts / high results'). The catalogue's amplification item is about engagement from inactive accounts, not timelines that only amplify. <https://medium.com/dfrlab/botspot-twelve-ways-to-spot-a-bot-aedc7d9c110c>
- **SA**: Gendered or sexualised threats against a woman journalist, and online abuse that leads to offline attacks *Why:* In the UNESCO/ICFJ study, 20% of women journalists surveyed were attacked offline in incidents that began online, and 18% received threats of sexual violence. The catalogue's online-abuse item is gender-neutral. <https://www.icfj.org/our-work/chilling-global-study-online-violence-against-women-journalists>
- **SA**: Interpol Red Notice or diffusion sought against the investigator by the subject's government (transnational repression) *Why:* Freedom House documents politically motivated Red Notices and diffusions as a tool of transnational repression. SA mentions states reaching critics abroad but not this mechanism. <https://freedomhouse.org/report/special-report/2026/collaboration-and-resistance-tracking-transnational-repression-2025>
- **SA**: Investigator or outlet designated a 'foreign agent', with labelling duties, frozen earnings and criminal liability *Why:* CPJ documents this administrative and financial pressure, separate from criminal defamation charges. Russia prosecuted or fined 18 journalists under it in late 2024, and El Salvador passed a similar law in 2025. <https://cpj.org/2025/02/russia-preps-to-block-income-of-foreign-agent-journalists/>
- **PP**: Polling-station results lost or missing from the tally, and constituency results showing near-100% turnout with near-unanimous votes *Why:* Carter Center observers in the DRC in 2011 flagged nearly 2,000 Kinshasa polling-station results as lost, and turnout of 99–100% in some Katanga constituencies. The catalogue covers statistical fingerprints but not missing results. <https://cartercenter.org/news/pr/drc-121011.html>

# Media suite: source verification, round 2

Oct 18, 2026 · @Ony

## What was checked and what differs

This round checked the media suite mockups' claims against primary sources: the live Hugging Face model cards and licence texts, W3C and IETF texts, US statutes and evidence rules, arXiv papers, PubMed and PMC studies, and NIST, C2PA and SWGDE documents. Each section below sets the mockup's wording beside what the source says, with a result of supported, partly, not addressed by the source, or contradicted. The mockup itself was not edited. After the first pass every section was re-read against its sources a second time, and rows, figures, quotes and results that did not hold were corrected in place. A third pass then settled the facts that had been left open: the jurisdiction rows on the Legal status board, Indiana's definition of electronic communication, the DIHARD III figures, the SAM licence from the official repository, the NIST hash documents, SynthID retention, the detector and re-save figures, ELA wording, and the wellbeing gaps on breaks, muting and Arabic dialects.

**Where the sources differ from the mockup**

- **File formats.** Four of the ten model slots ship formats the mockup does not list: Whisper small (CTranslate2 `model.bin`), Omnilingual ASR (`.pt`), diarization (`.bin`) and translation (a 5.48 GB `pytorch_model.bin`). The mockup says safetensors for all four, and its own rule refuses pickle formats unless a reason is written.
- **Separator licence.** The SAM License text has no non-commercial clause, in both the third-party copy and the official repository's LICENSE file. It has trade-control and end-use limits, and the gate needs manual approval.
- **Diarization gate.** The pyannote gate is a contact form, not a use term. WhisperX 3.8.6 defaults to community-1, not 3.1.
- **Word timing.** WhisperX has 41 alignment languages, not about 40, and French, German, Spanish, Italian and Vietnamese models are CC BY-NC 4.0.
- **Vision model.** If it is Qwen2.5-VL 7B, the model is Apache-2.0, so an amber licence light would not come from the licence.
- **Delete and key destruction.** Full-disk encryption alone is not sanitization, and key destruction counts only when every key copy is destroyed and no plain data sat outside the encrypted area.
- **Credential states.** The C2PA specification has Well-Formed, Valid and Trusted as manifest states: "None found" is outside its state model, and "Cannot check" exists only as status codes such as manifest.inaccessible, not as a manifest state.
- **Language detection.** "Detected per file, so a switch to Arabic is found" is contradicted if the app gives one language label per file.
- **Wellbeing.** Blur and greyscale not protecting is supported by two larger trials. Interactive, user-controlled blur is supported with limits by one online-worker study (Das 2020, outside PubMed), and blind review has one forensic field study behind it (Mattijssen 2020). The card that says preparation helps is not tested by the generic-warning trials, though one interview study has a matching theme. "Sound is the strongest" risk factor is not shown by anything retrieved. Breaks and muting were measured only in one cross-sectional survey of 500 investigators and examiners (Mitchell 2023), with small or non-significant associations after adjustment.
- **Law.** Indiana's journalist defence covers only the civil claim and only persons described in IC 34-46-4-1. Proposed Rule 707 was withdrawn from final approval in May 2026, and draft Rule 901(c) has never been published for comment. In the Legal status jurisdiction rows, Nevada's NRS 179.463 pointer is misleading (it is a peace-officer exception), the Canada and UK rows are imprecise, and the Michigan, Washington and Germany rows match the statutes.
- **Standards.** RFC 6962 is obsoleted by RFC 9162. "Daily" checkpoints are not in the RFC. A fully offline time-stamp check cannot confirm certificate revocation. New BagIt bags SHOULD default to SHA-512, and the mockup uses SHA-256.
- **Figures not traced.** The detector figures (79%, 61%, 38%) could not be matched to any table in Deepfake-Eval-2024 or AIGIBench. The re-save rate (0.57 to 1.00) is the range in BG-REAL (arXiv 2607.26232, a 2026 preprint) for re-encoded authentic images across baselines. The "66% and 9%" operating point matches TruFor Table 12. The SynthID Detector retention line is contradicted by Google's privacy notice, which says uploaded files are deleted once results return and a digital signature is kept for 24 hours.

**Where the sources agree with the mockup**

- The pyannote 4.0.7 metrics default, the endpoint and the `PYANNOTE_METRICS_ENABLED` switch.
- The NLLB 1.3B non-commercial label.
- The Media Fragments syntax, the cited WCAG criteria (1.4.2, 2.3.1, 2.5.7, 2.5.8), and the SHA-256 and hash-before-and-after handling.
- The federal one-party-consent rule and Indiana's Level 5 felony and civil damages.
- Watermark-found and no-credentials wording, and the "no fake-or-real verdict" position.

## Gates and licence texts that need a decision

**SAM Audio (the separator).** The [SAM License](https://huggingface.co/audio-cpp/SAM-Audio-GGUF/raw/main/LICENSE) (last updated 19 Nov 2025) grants a royalty-free, worldwide, non-transferable licence to use, copy, modify and distribute. A search of the full text for "commercial" finds nothing, so the mockup's "non-commercial" label has no basis in this text. What it does contain:

- Use must follow trade-control law and may not involve ITAR activities or end uses the controls prohibit, "military or warfare purposes", espionage, or weapons. How "espionage" applies to investigative reporting is a question for a lawyer; the text does not define it.
- Use must follow privacy and data-protection law. No reverse engineering. Redistribution must carry a copy of the licence. Research publications must acknowledge the use.
- Meta may change the terms at any time, and continued use counts as agreement. Suing Meta over the materials or their outputs ends the licence. On termination the user must delete the materials. California law and courts govern.
- The gate on [facebook/sam-audio-base](https://huggingface.co/facebook/sam-audio-base) is manual approval. The form asks first and last name, date of birth, country, affiliation, job title (the list includes "Reporter"), and IP location, and the user ticks a box agreeing that Meta may store, process and share the answers under its privacy policy.
- The official LICENSE file in the facebookresearch/sam-audio repository (last updated 19 Nov 2025, Meta Platforms Ireland Limited or Meta Platforms, Inc.) was read from raw.githubusercontent.com and has the same grant and restrictions. It adds "nuclear industries or applications" to the prohibited end uses, and it has no non-commercial clause, user-count threshold or field-of-use limit beyond those listed. The repository README says to request access to the checkpoints on the Hugging Face repo facebook/sam-audio-large and to authenticate with a token. The Hub licence tag reads sam-license.

**pyannote (the diarization).** The 3.1 pipeline and segmentation-3.0 are MIT. Their gate is automatic and asks for company and website; its stated purpose is a user survey plus occasional emails about paid pyannote services. The newer [community-1 pipeline](https://huggingface.co/pyannote/speaker-diarization-community-1) is CC-BY-4.0 (attribution required), asks for company and use case, and is what WhisperX 3.8.6 uses by default, not 3.1. The Hub's own [gated-models page](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/models-gated) says every gated model needs the user to share username and email with the authors, and the raw README of both pipelines refuses unauthenticated reads. The pyannote-audio repository README (main and develop identical; repository licence MIT) has setup step 3 as accepting the user conditions of speaker-diarization-community-1 on Hugging Face and step 4 as creating an access token. It calls 3.1 the "legacy" pipeline, says community-1 improves speaker counting and assignment, and describes precision-2 as the premium pyannoteAI pipeline that runs on pyannoteAI servers with an API key. The README does not say what access 3.1 needs. Its telemetry is described as optional, anonymous usage metrics (pipeline name or origin class, file duration, number of speakers; nothing identifying the user) and is controlled with PYANNOTE\_METRICS\_ENABLED or set\_telemetry\_metrics; the README does not state the default value, which I did not check in the code.

**Omnilingual ASR.** Apache-2.0 on the [model card](https://huggingface.co/facebook/omniASR-CTC-1B). The CTC 1B model has 975 million parameters; the LLM 1B model has 2.28 billion and an 8.5 GiB download. The official files are `.pt`. The Hub's [pickle-scanning page](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/security-pickle) states that PyTorch's default format is pickle and that loading one can run arbitrary code.

**Translation.** NLLB-200 1.3B and its distilled twin carry the CC-BY-NC-4.0 tag, consistent with the mockup's red light. By licence tag only (training-data terms not checked): [M2M100 1.2B](https://huggingface.co/facebook/m2m100_1.2B) is MIT with a 4.96 GB `.bin`; [MADLAD-400 3B](https://huggingface.co/google/madlad400-3b-mt) is Apache-2.0 with an 11.76 GB safetensors file; [opus-mt-ar-en](https://huggingface.co/Helsinki-NLP/opus-mt-ar-en) is Apache-2.0 with a `.bin`; [SeamlessM4T v2](https://huggingface.co/facebook/seamless-m4t-v2-large) is CC-BY-NC-4.0.

## Telemetry and package checks

The mockup's claim about pyannote's usage metrics holds, and the switch works as described. Source read: the pyannote.audio 4.0.7 wheel from PyPI (the newest release listed on 18 Oct 2026) and the WhisperX 3.8.6 and faster-whisper 1.2.1 wheels (WhisperX 3.8.6 requires faster-whisper 1.2.0 or newer).

- **Default is on.** The package's own `config.yaml` sets `metrics_enabled: true` and an export endpoint at `otel.pyannote.ai`, a pyannote.ai host.
- **What is recorded.** Per call: pipeline name and origin, package version, a random per-session ID, the audio file's duration in seconds, and the requested speaker counts. The code records no file path and no audio.
- **The switch.** `PYANNOTE_METRICS_ENABLED` counts as on only for "true" or "1"; "0" turns it off. The module fills in the variable from the config only if it is not already set when the package is imported, so the variable must exist before the import. `set_telemetry_metrics(False, save_choice_as_default=True)` also writes the choice into the config file.
- **WhisperX depends on it.** WhisperX 3.8.6 requires `pyannote-audio>=4.0.0`, plus `huggingface-hub<1.0.0` and `torch~=2.8.0`.
- **Different default model.** WhisperX 3.8.6 names `pyannote/speaker-diarization-community-1` as its diarization model, not 3.1.
- **Silence filter.** faster-whisper 1.2.1 does its voice-activity filtering with a Silero ONNX file bundled inside the package, so that step downloads nothing from Hugging Face. Its model table points to the Systran repos used in the first table.
- **Hugging Face Hub switches.** `HF_HUB_DISABLE_TELEMETRY=1` turns off the usage data that `transformers` and other HF libraries send. `HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1` stops all Hub calls, and the Hub docs say that without it, `hf_hub_download` still makes an HTTP request to check for a newer file even when the file is cached. The `hf` command-line tool also checks PyPI for updates at startup unless `HF_HUB_DISABLE_UPDATE_CHECK` is set.

**Recording and interception**

Federal: [18 U.S.C. 2510(4)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2510) and [2511](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2511). Section 2511(1) prohibits intercepting a wire, oral or electronic communication; 2510(4) defines intercept. It is not unlawful for a person not acting under color of law to intercept where they are a party or one party consented, unless the purpose is a criminal or tortious act. The penalty is up to five years.

Indiana: [IC 35-33.5](https://iga.in.gov/ic/2026/Title_35/Article_33.5.pdf). "Interception" (35-31.5-2-176, official text at iga.in.gov, which applies it for purposes of IC 35-33.5) means the intentional recording or acquiring the contents of an electronic communication by a person who is neither sender nor receiver and without consent of the sender or receiver; the definition names electronic communication and not in-person speech. A knowing or intentional violation is a Level 5 felony (35-33.5-5-5). The civil action gives the greater of actual damages, $100 per day or $1,000, plus costs, fees and, where the court finds it appropriate, punitive damages, within two years (35-33.5-5-4). Section 5-4(c) gives a person described in IC 34-46-4-1 an affirmative defence to the civil action if they were unaware of the unlawful interception, did not intercept or procure it, and used a communication for the purpose of assisting them to independently confirm information. That shield-law section lists owners and editorial or reportorial employees of newspapers, periodicals, press associations, wire services and licensed broadcasters who receive income from news gathering; it does not mention independents. "Electronic communication" (35-31.5-2-110, for purposes of IC 35-33.5) means any transfer of signs, signals, writing, images, sounds, data or oral communication transmitted in whole or in part by a wire, a radio, or an electromagnetic, photoelectronic or photo-optical system. Oral communication is covered only when a transmission system carries it; face-to-face speech that is not transmitted is outside the definition, and the interception definition separately requires a person other than the sender or receiver acting without their consent.

**Biometric identifiers**

**What the sources say that bears on the mockup**

- **Deletion and encryption (NIST 800-88 Rev. 2).** Rev. 2 mentions deleting file pointers only as an example for devices that offer just a factory reset, where rewriting is not supported; it does not rank it as the weakest form of "clear". Overwrite on flash with wear levelling cannot reach all data. Cryptographic erase counts as a purge only if no sensitive plaintext was ever on the media before the keys were set up, key strength is at least 128 bits, a validated module is used (a requirement on federal agencies, section 3.2.2), and every copy of the keys is zeroized. It "should not be trusted" where keys were backed up or escrowed, unless the organization has a high level of confidence in how and where the keys were stored and managed outside the system. Partial sanitization carries risks and whole-media sanitization is preferred; partial cryptographic erase is possible when the interface can sanitize a subset of keys and there is confidence the data was stored only in areas that can be reliably sanitized. Owners are strongly advised to consult records offices on retention first; verification results should be inspected and the certificate of sanitization completed, though Rev. 2 removed almost all of the verification language and says cryptographic erase's effectiveness does not depend on documentation.
- **Hashes.** SHA-2 and SHA-3 are "acceptable for all hash function applications" in 800-131A Rev. 2. The Rev. 3 draft keeps SHA-256 and marks SHA-1 disallowed after 2030 for applying protection in non-signature uses (its Table 12); processing already-protected data and verifying signatures become legacy use.
- **Evidence handling (SP 800-86, SWGDE 17-I-001 and 18-V-001).** Hash the original before and the copy after copying, compare, keep a custody log, work on a copy, document tools and settings. SWGDE adds that a hash "cannot demonstrate the veracity of the scene depicted in the image" (18-I-001).
- **C2PA.** A hard binding is a hash that identifies the whole asset or a portion of it and matches only that asset; a soft binding is not statistically unique, being either computed from the content (a fingerprint) or carried as an invisible watermark, and can match renditions (2.3.12, 2.3.13). Allowed hashes are SHA2-256, SHA2-384 and SHA2-512. A manifest is Well-Formed, then Valid, then Trusted. The three manifest states have no "cannot check" member, but the specification defines could-not-check outcomes as status codes (manifest.inaccessible for an unreachable remote manifest, algorithm.unsupported, and an informational code when an OCSP responder gave no response). Its explainer says provenance "alone cannot tell you whether the digital content is true". The Interim Trust List was frozen on 1 Jan 2026. The Conformance Program and the official C2PA Trust List launched in mid-2025.
- **Detectors and watermarks (NIST AI 100-4).** Image detectors score about 50 to 70% in cross-generator tests and do worse after compression or resizing. Video detectors report 62 to 99% and audio detectors 50% to above 90%. Most current watermarking schemes "have been consistently found vulnerable to removal".

Simplification Concept Lab

Five ways to show less at once without losing anything. These are pictures to react to, not the app: Kestrel case data is fictional. Each one is set up so its animation tells you what changed, and each has a still-frame fallback.

MotionTurns every animation here into an instant change, as the Workbench's own Calm motion does.

A. The part you're on opens, the rest fold to a spine

Check has four sections. Only the one you're in is open; the others shrink to one line that still carries their count and state. An event can pulse a spine but never steals the screen.

B. One record, four depths

Every card keeps who, what, how much, when and where from at its shallowest depth. Hover peeks; a click opens it in place; Enter opens the full room. Nothing is a new window until you ask for the whole thing.

C. Zoom changes what a list is, not just how big it is

Fifteen thousand proposals can't be a list. At the top it is six counted groups; one zoom in, the groups show their rows; one more, it is the records. All counts in this lab are invented for the picture. The line "found · shown · why" is always on screen.

Altitude

D. One switch for how much you see, with a floor it cannot go below

The Red Flags screen at three levels. The outlined parts are the ethical floor: level, "indicator, not a finding", innocent explanations. They are there at every level. What folds away says how many things it holds and offers them back. The nudge is an offer only.

E. Buttons arrive when something is selected

The Library has about forty-four fixed buttons today. Here a row shows its two likely actions on hover; selecting rows raises one bar with only the actions that make sense for that selection, then it leaves.

## A citation checked and deliberately handled with care

**"Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence"** (the popular phrase behind
standard Rule 8, which itself reads "Extraordinary claims require commensurately strong
evidence"; Module 09 rule 4, not Rule 8, handles minority positions) is popularly
attributed to Carl Sagan, who used the phrase on the *Cosmos* television
series (1980). Checked against Quote Investigator's sourcing work: the
underlying principle is older — it traces through Pierre-Simon Laplace's
writing on the weight of testimony versus the strangeness of a claim, and a
closely related phrasing ("an extraordinary claim requires extraordinary
proof") was used by sociologist Marcello Truzzi in 1978, before Sagan's
popularization. RAS cites this as "the Sagan standard, popularized via
*Cosmos* (1980), building on an older evidentiary principle traceable to
Laplace and articulated similarly by Truzzi (1978)" rather than crediting
Sagan with originating the underlying idea — the same attribution
discipline DAS's source index applied when it checked a specific claim
against a named author's actual writing before citing it.

## Cross-references to sibling systems

RS-04 through RS-06 overlap in subject matter with DAS's cognitive-bias
citations (DS-06, DS-08, DS-09) and LAS's reasoning-error citations
(LS-04), but are cited here for a distinct proposition each time —
replication and study-design rigor, not decision bias or a specific
reasoning fallacy — so no duplicate citation was needed across the
Decision Authority System's (DAS) or the Logic Authority System's (LAS)
source index. Consult those indexes directly
for the decision-science and formal/informal-fallacy literature rather
than re-deriving it here.

## Limits and gaps

Limits 1 and 2 came from the disconfirming searches; limits 3 and 4 are gaps found in
reading.

1. **Public-interest tests are retro-fitted, and the hard part is proportionality.** The
   main criticism is not that the public interest cannot be defined but that it is fitted
   to stories after publication, and that weighing it is the difficult step (ES-08).
   Bounds Module 01 rules 3 and 4 and Module 04 rule 2: the reasoning and the weighing are
   written and dated before publication, and a named category alone does not count.
   Tests H01, H03.
2. **"Do no harm" stated absolutely conflicts with the codes.** ES-06 states it as an
   absolute; ES-02 and ES-01 accept harm that is unavoidable and proportionate. Bounds the
   Standard §2.3 and Module 02 rule 7: EHS uses proportionality with recorded reasons, and
   rejecting a story only because some harm remains fails the rule. Test H04.
3. **"The decision stays with a person" is supported only indirectly.** No source read
   states it as a rule for model-assisted work. ES-09 places hard decisions at the highest
   editorial level, ES-01 makes the editor show the reasoning, and ES-11 cautions against
   unsupervised model use in legal tasks, a different field. Bounds the Standard §2.1,
   Module 01 rule 9, Module 03 rule 9 and Module 04 rules 6–7: the rule is a design choice
   of this system resting on that indirect support. Test H09.
4. **Source-data minimisation rests on two sources.** ES-10 states metadata scrubbing,
   limiting who receives files and deletion on request; ES-04 establishes data retention,
   handover and metadata as exposure routes but does not itself recommend minimisation.
   No measured effect of either was found. Bounds Module 03 rules 4–6. Test H06.

5. **The law protects more than confidential sources.** Indiana's shield law protects a
   newsgatherer's "source" without reference to confidentiality (ES-23), and US law and the IPSO
   code set different ages for a minor (ES-19, ES-20, ES-01). Bounds Standard §8: its terms say
   when this system's steps apply; they do not state what any law protects.
